# 03 — VX-02 Direction

- **ID / status:** `VX-02 — PROPOSED`
- **Evaluation identity:** blind; genealogy intentionally withheld
- **Evidence ceiling:** no approved real-world Toplink people/place/process material

## 1. Visual thesis

**Nhịp Hỏi — Đáp Rõ:** Toplink behaves as a sequence of answerable human questions. Each movement
pairs one visitor concern with one bounded response—an explanation, a real encounter, a scope, or a
truthful next state—so clarity and accountability never need a clinical or administrative costume.

Toplink needs this because its core repair is not more authority styling; it is a reliable path from
ordinary discomfort and uncertainty to a next step the interface can actually support.

## 2. Composition

- Seven unequal exchanges: arrival question; editable orientation; limit; real-world encounter;
  service scope; visit expectation; action status plus learning exit.
- Each exchange uses a clear question anchor and a larger response zone, but changes ratio and
  placement according to content.
- A real people/place image can take over the response zone when trust becomes salient.
- Service and visit chapters use long horizontal spans, not repeated boxes.
- The action chapter reverses the visual balance: consequence becomes dominant and the CTA becomes
  secondary until review is complete.

## 3. Hierarchy

Visitor questions are the navigation anchors. The most important response is the plain boundary or
verified fact; system state and action come next. Ownership/review details stay behind deliberate
disclosure unless essential to a claim. Hierarchy is therefore comprehension-first, not metadata-first.

## 4. Typography behavior

One highly legible sans system carries display, reading and controls, differentiated through width,
weight, line length and placement rather than prestige contrast. Question anchors are compact and
firm; answers are larger but calmer. Public annotations stay normal-sized and sentence case. New
font families are neither assumed nor required at this gate.

## 5. Density

Density changes by answer type: sparse for questions and limits; rich for a verified human/place
encounter; structured for service comparison; concise for action state. No movement has high
density merely to communicate expertise.

## 6. Whitespace

Whitespace separates a visitor question from what Toplink can responsibly answer. A larger pause
precedes evidence and action, signalling a change in consequence. The rhythm is deliberate but not
luxurious: empty space never replaces useful context.

## 7. Image behavior

Photography is a response, not atmosphere. A place image answers “Tôi sẽ đến đâu?”, a person-in-role
image answers “Ai có vai trò công khai?”, and a process image answers “Một bước diễn ra thế nào?”.
Contextual wide views precede closer detail. If no image can answer a real question, it does not
appear.

## 8. Human-evidence behavior

Real place, person and service facts enter in the same question-response rhythm as all other
content; they are not isolated as social proof. The visitor sees the minimum fact needed to judge
the next decision. Source, consent and review remain operational requirements, while missing data
causes omission or a plain unresolved state.

## 9. Color behavior

The base is a high-contrast neutral reading field. One warm action color marks active choice and
decision, while a cooler functional contrast may distinguish explanation from action if accessibility
and verified Toplink fit support it. Large areas remain quiet; no hue stands in for truth, tradition
or expertise. Exact values remain open.

## 10. Interaction shape

Orientation uses editable sentence starters rather than diagnostic categories. Disclosure opens the
answer to a question in place. Service scope is navigated through labelled spans; action state is a
full-width review zone with edit, retry and remain-uncertain paths. No chat bubbles, dashboard
controls or decorative progress trail.

## 11. Surface behavior

Flat contiguous fields and decisive changes in alignment define chapters. Some response zones may
use a tonal field for scope or state, but there is no simulated material, stacked depth or repeated
container chrome. The signature survives in grayscale.

## 12. Motion expression

- **KHAI:** a response zone becomes available after its question, without delaying content.
- **DẪN:** focus and state move from phrase to boundary to next choice.
- **TỤ:** selected language and scope gather into one editable review.
- **AN:** evidence, limits and errors remain stable; recovery changes only the needed state.

Motion is local, sparse and fully replaced by immediate state under reduced motion.

## 13. Trust expression

Trust comes from the repeated discipline “question asked → bounded answer given.” Unsupported facts
never receive a polished substitute. People/place evidence sits before deeper service choice;
limitations are visually equal to positive scope; action status states what has and has not happened.

## 14. Mobile translation

Question anchors become full-width headings followed by their response; ratios collapse without
creating a stack of identical cards. The action review remains before its control. Images keep
context-first order, text never overlays busy photography, and all states preserve native reading
and focus order.

## 15. Service discovery

Service discovery begins with “Toplink có thể hỗ trợ trong phạm vi nào?” and answers through a few
reviewed scope spans. Each span shows a limit and an optional detail path. No service is selected
from the visitor's initial phrase; uncertainty remains a peer route.

## 16. Knowledge bridge

`Tôi muốn hiểu thêm` appears as an alternate answer where a term or boundary requires explanation.
It leads to one reviewed practical page with a source/review note in the content model, then offers
a safe return. Visual treatment remains identical to other answer zones.

## 17. CTA / consequence expression

Before any primary action becomes visually dominant, the response zone says what the action can do
now. In a local-only state it says nothing was sent. With future verified transport, it names the
recipient/channel, current state, edit path, retry and human handoff. The control label matches the
actual consequence.

## Risks and hard-fail triggers

- Risk: repetition of question/response pairs can become a new metronome.
- Risk: excessive neutrality can feel cold before real people/place imagery exists.
- Hard fail: high-density operational labels, clinical styling, decorative evidence signals,
  identical exchange geometry, fabricated facts, unclear state or inaccessible controls.

## H4R tests if selected

Test three different exchange ratios to prevent repetition; verify warmth with no approved image;
test a real-evidence specimen when available; test that consequence outranks CTA; and test mobile,
keyboard, 200% zoom and reduced-motion parity.
