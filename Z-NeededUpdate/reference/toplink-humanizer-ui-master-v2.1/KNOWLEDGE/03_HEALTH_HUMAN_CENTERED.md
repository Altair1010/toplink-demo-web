# Human-Centered Health UX Evidence

Human-centered work begins with actual needs/context and iterative prototyping, rather than choosing an aesthetic first.  
**Nguồn:** [WHO HCD guidance](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/digital-health-documents/scaling-innovations-in-public-health-systems_who-guidance-and-toolkit.pdf)

NHS guidance explicitly asks teams to understand clinical, practical and emotional needs.  
**Nguồn:** [NHS user needs](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/standards-and-technology/service-standard-points/1-understand-users-and-their-needs-context-health-and-care)

Health Literacy Online recommends realistic, relevant imagery and plain language; imagery should support comprehension rather than decorate.  
**Nguồn:** [Health Literacy Online — images](https://odphp.health.gov/healthliteracyonline/display/section-3-8/)  
**Nguồn:** [Health Literacy Online — full guidance](https://odphp.health.gov/healthliteracyonline/full/)

Trust/credibility reviews of online health information repeatedly identify interface design, clear layout, authority/ownership and ease of use as relevant cues.  
**Nguồn:** [JMIR trust review](https://www.jmir.org/2017/6/e218/)

Patient-centered communication is positively associated with trust in a recent telehealth study, supporting explicit “what happens next / who responds” interaction copy.  
**Nguồn:** [JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e63295)

W3C provides reduced-motion guidance for animation triggered by interaction; Humanizer motion must have a reduced/static path.  
**Nguồn:** [W3C animation from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)
