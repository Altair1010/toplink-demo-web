# Humanizer Thesis

## Working model

`Humanizer = Specificity × Evidence × Legibility × Trust × Narrative × Interaction Consequence`

Đây là project model, không phải validated psychometric model.

## Why these dimensions

Human-centered design guidance from WHO frames solution development around understanding user needs/behaviors, prototyping, experimentation and iteration.  
**Nguồn:** [WHO human-centred design guidance](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/digital-health-documents/scaling-innovations-in-public-health-systems_who-guidance-and-toolkit.pdf)

NHS health-service guidance requires teams to understand clinical, practical and emotional needs in context.  
**Nguồn:** [NHS — understand users and their needs](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/standards-and-technology/service-standard-points/1-understand-users-and-their-needs-context-health-and-care)

A meta-analysis of 147 studies on web-based health information found navigability, aesthetics and ease of understanding among the strongest antecedents of perceived information quality; therefore “human” must not be achieved by sacrificing clarity or visual coherence.  
**Nguồn:** [JMIR 2022 meta-analysis](https://www.jmir.org/2022/4/e36463/)

## Eight layers

1. **User Reality** — needs/context before aesthetics.
2. **Human Evidence** — real people/place/process/material.
3. **Professional Specificity** — domain language with plain-language bridge.
4. **Editorial Narrative** — attention sequence, not component collection.
5. **Material Character** — digital identity derived from physical evidence.
6. **Interaction Consequence** — who/what happens next is visible.
7. **Motion Meaning** — motion expresses narrative/state/spatial relation.
8. **Trust Verification** — provenance, accessibility, state completeness, tests.

## Non-goal

Humanizer is not “make it intentionally imperfect”. In a health context, unclear navigation, unreadable typography or arbitrary asymmetry are defects, not authenticity.  
**Nguồn:** [JMIR 2022 meta-analysis](https://www.jmir.org/2022/4/e36463/)
